# 20 Practice Questions


For every question:

1. State the null and alternative hypotheses in terms of the population parameter.
2. Decide whether the test is left-tailed, right-tailed or two-tailed, and explain why.
3. Check the assumptions needed for a z test, calculate the standard error and z statistic, and find the p-value.
4. Make a decision at the stated significance level and write a conclusion in the context of the question. Distinguish insufficient evidence from evidence that the null hypothesis is true.
5. Complete any additional task included in the question.

Use **only z tests**. Treat every stated benchmark as a fixed population value, rather than an estimate from another sample. All samples are randomly selected, observations are independent, and any finite population is much larger than its sample. Whenever a population standard deviation is supplied, assume that it is known and remains applicable to the population being tested. For percentages, work with proportions in the calculations. Use the null-hypothesis proportion to calculate the standard error; do not apply a continuity correction. Do not round intermediate calculations; report the final z statistic to three decimal places and the p-value to four decimal places, retaining additional precision when a decision is close to the cutoff.

## Question 1

Kalpa's paid membership renewal benchmark is **72%**. Following a change in onboarding, a random sample of **600 members who have reached their renewal date** contains **408 renewals**.

The membership head suspects that the renewal rate has **fallen below the benchmark**. Test this suspicion at the **5% significance level**.

Also report the difference between the observed renewal rate and the benchmark in **percentage points**. Explain why members who have not yet reached their renewal date should not be included in this sample.

## Question 2

An online retailer's population mean order-packing time is **42 minutes**, with a known population standard deviation of **12 minutes**. After introducing a new packing layout, a random sample of **144 orders** has a mean packing time of **39.8 minutes**.

The operations manager wants evidence that the new layout has **reduced the population mean packing time**. Test this claim at the **1% significance level**.

The manager says, “The sample average is lower, so the improvement is proved.” Evaluate that statement using your result.

## Question 3

The buyer will escalate the supplier only if there is evidence that the population defect rate **exceeds 2%**. Conduct the test at the **1% significance level**.

Calculate the expected number of defective items under the null hypothesis. Explain why comparing 47 with that expected count alone is not enough to make the decision.

## Question 4

Quality control wants to know whether the population mean fill volume has **changed in either direction**, rather than testing only for overfilling. Use a **5% significance level**.

Give both critical z values. Explain why choosing a right-tailed test after noticing that the sample mean is above 500 mL would change the original question.

## Question 14

## Question 20

A fulfilment process has a population mean task time of **60 seconds**, with known population standard deviation **15 seconds**. After an equipment adjustment, a random sample of **225 tasks** has a mean time of **62.1 seconds**.

Test whether the population mean task time **differs from 60 seconds**, at the **5% significance level**.

For the same sample size and known standard deviation, determine the boundary values of the **sample mean** separating rejection from non-rejection under this two-tailed test. Show where 62.1 seconds falls relative to these boundaries. Use rejection when the p-value is strictly less than the significance level; equality belongs to the non-rejection region. Do not interpret the non-rejection region as proof that the population mean is unchanged.
